# Inbunden clears every check but cannot be owned at home

Inbunden can work as the brand, but it is the wrong choice for a Sweden-first company that means to sell physical hardcover books: the name is commercially untouched (no company, app, shop or mark anywhere; inbunden.com, .app, .io and .net all unregistered), yet Swedish trade-mark law will refuse it as a word mark for exactly the goods that matter (class 16 photo books, class 40 printing and binding, class 35 retail), because "inbunden" is the everyday Swedish word for "hardcover" and appears as a retail filter on Adlibris and Bokus and as a product name at BoD and Saal Digital ([Adlibris](https://www.adlibris.com/sv/kundservice/fragor/information-om-vara-bokformat-och-bandtyper); [BoD](https://www.bod.se/bok/inbunden-bok); [Saal Digital](https://www.saal-digital.se/fotobok/premium-presentask)). Under EUTMR Art. 7(2) the Swedish meaning alone is enough to refuse an EU-wide mark for those goods, and Bolagsverket applies the same distinctiveness test to "Inbunden AB" ([IPPT, Art. 7 EUTMR](https://www.ippt.eu/legal-texts/eu-trade-mark-regulation-2017/article-7); [lagen.nu 2018:1653](https://lagen.nu/2018:1653)). The paradox is that the same word is a strong, arbitrary mark everywhere Swedish is not understood, under the CJEU's Matratzen Concord rule, so the brand would be weakest in its launch market and strongest abroad, where it collides with the English word "inbound" and HubSpot's INBOUND mark ([ipcuria, C-421/04](https://ipcuria.eu/case?reference=C-421%2F04); [Justia, INBOUND](https://trademarks.justia.com/866/31/inbound-86631432.html)). Swedes will also hear the second dictionary sense, "reserved, withdrawn, introvert", listed with as many synonyms as the book sense ([Synonymer.se](https://www.synonymer.se/sv-syn/inbunden)). No Nordic brand built on a literal category word has gone global; the precedents that did (Fjällräven, Hövding, IKEA's product names) all use words unrelated to their product, and Klarna dropped its descriptive name Kreditor before scaling ([Wikipedia, Klarna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klarna)). The recommendation is therefore to keep Feedbound as the master brand and, if the Swedish word is wanted, use "Inbunden" as the name of the hardcover edition, or accept a logo-only registration in 16/40 and a contestable word mark in 9/42 at roughly SEK 3,700 (PRV) or EUR 900 (EUIPO) in official fees ([PRV fees](https://www.prv.se/sv/varumarke/forbered-dig-infor-varumarkesansokan/avgifter-for-varumarke/); [Abrande](https://abrande.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-register-a-trademark-in-sweden-or-the-eu-prv-and-euipo-fees-compared-for-2026)). TMview, PRV's database, EUIPO eSearch and Bolagsverket could not be queried this round; the register position rests on the 27 September 2026 TMview pass that returned zero rows for "Inbunden" in Contains and Fuzzy mode ([TMview](https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/#/tmview/results?page=1&pageSize=100&criteria=C&basicSearch=Inbunden)).

## The book trade sells "inbunden"; the photo-book trade mostly says "hårt omslag"

"Inbunden" is not jargon. Adlibris's customer-service glossary defines it as "hård pärm, vanligen sydd i ryggen, ofta med skyddsomslag", alongside Pocket, Häftad, Danskt band, Flexband and Kartonnage, and uses the identical text on its Finland-Swedish and B2B sites ([Adlibris](https://www.adlibris.com/sv/kundservice/fragor/information-om-vara-bokformat-och-bandtyper)). Bokus exposes it as a URL-level filter value (`binding_normalized=inbunden`) with a category page titled "Inbunden - Svenska - Böcker" ([Bokus](https://classic.bokus.com/cgi-bin/product_search.cgi?language=Svenska&binding_normalized=inbunden)). Print-on-demand suppliers sell it as a product: BoD's page "Inbunden bok" explains that "en inbunden bok har ett fast, stabilt omslag" and that such books "utstrålar alltid en särskild kvalitet" ([BoD](https://www.bod.se/bok/inbunden-bok)); HelloPrint sells "inbundna böcker"; the consumer guide booki.se describes it as the most durable format, released first and priced at a premium ([HelloPrint](https://www.helloprint.com/sv-se/inbundnaboecker); [Booki](https://booki.se/guider/inbunden-pocket-haftad)). Any Swede who buys books knows the word, and every Swedish printer and bookseller needs to keep using it, which is precisely the "frihållningsbehov" (need to keep free) that PRV and the Patent and Market Court of Appeal weigh against registration.

Inside the photo-book segment the picture is split, and this is the one point where the earlier round-one screening and this round's check diverge. The two largest players do not use the word at all: CEWE.se calls hardcover "Hårt omslag" and binding "Klassisk bokbindning", and Smartphoto.se sells "Hårt omslag", "Hårt omslag linne" and "Hårt omslag läderimitation" ([CEWE](https://www.cewe.se/cewe-fotobok/papperstyper-och-omslag.html); [Smartphoto](https://www.smartphoto.se/fotobok)). Optimalprint uses it once as an adjective ("Välj en fotobok som är inbunden med hårt eller mjukt omslag") while labelling SKUs "Hårt omslag" ([Optimalprint](https://www.optimalprint.se/fotobocker)); Saal Digital, FindPenguins and My Social Book, however, do use "inbunden fotobok" as a plain product descriptor, and Popsa's English page carries a localised title "kvadratisk, inbunden fotobok" ([Saal Digital](https://www.saal-digital.se/fotobok/premium-presentask); [FindPenguins](https://findpenguins.com/travelbook); [Popsa](https://popsa.com/en-us/products/photo-books/hardcover-photo-book/square/)). The Norwegian and Danish cognates "innbundet" and "indbundet" are used the same way by fotoknudsen.no, Peleman and Pinterest listings ([fotoknudsen](https://www.fotoknudsen.no/layflat-innbinding); [Peleman](https://peleman.no/produkter/innbindingsbok/)). The practical reading is that "Inbunden" as a photo-book brand is a book-trade word transplanted into a segment that has half-standardised on "hårt omslag": distinctive enough to feel like a name in a CEWE-style shop, generic enough that a trade-mark examiner will find "inbunden fotobok" on the first page of results.

The second sense is fully alive. Synonymer.se lists the personality meaning, "som inte visar sina känslor", with the synonyms "sluten, tyst, tystlåten, reserverad, otillgänglig, svårtillgänglig, tillknäppt, fåordig, inåtvänd, introvert", and illustrates it with "Han var inbunden och hade svårt att uttrycka sig" and "en flicka som sitter ensam och inbunden i ett hörn" ([Synonymer.se](https://www.synonymer.se/sv-syn/inbunden)). The exact SAOL and SO wording and sense ordering could not be retrieved (svenska.se returned only its navigation shell) and remain unverified ([SAOL](https://svenska.se/saol/?sok=inbunden&pz=4)). For the brand this double meaning is a genuine creative asset ("your private, bound-in life") and a mild liability, since the adjective applied to a person is negative (withdrawn, hard to reach); Swedish copywriters would have to lean into the pun deliberately rather than let it sit. A further Swedish-only cost is search: retail listings for "inbunden fotobok" and the Bokus and Adlibris filter pages will outrank a new brand for the bare word for a long time.

## Swedish and EU law refuse the word for books and binding, and may object for software

Both statutes bar a sign that describes the goods. Varumärkeslagen 2 kap. 5 § requires that a registered mark "måste ha särskiljningsförmåga för de varor eller tjänster som det avser", and 1 kap. 5 § denies distinctiveness to a sign consisting solely of elements that "i handeln visar varans eller tjänstens art, kvalitet, kvantitet, avsedda användning, värde, geografiska ursprung eller andra egenskaper" or that "i dagligt språkbruk eller enligt branschens vedertagna handelsbruk kommit att bli en sedvanlig beteckning för varan eller tjänsten" ([Riksdagen, SFS 2010:1877](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/varumarkeslag-20101877_sfs-2010-1877/)). EUTMR Art. 7(1)(c) refuses marks that "consist exclusively of signs or indications which may serve, in trade, to designate the kind, quality, quantity, intended purpose, value ... or other characteristics of the goods or service", and Art. 7(2) adds that this applies "notwithstanding that the grounds of non-registrability obtain in only part of the Union" ([IPPT, Art. 7 EUTMR](https://www.ippt.eu/legal-texts/eu-trade-mark-regulation-2017/article-7)). EUIPO's guidelines make the test whether the meaning is "immediately perceived by the relevant public as providing information about the goods", with a link that is "sufficiently direct and specific" and "understood without further reflection", assessed in all official EU languages using dictionary entries and internet evidence of descriptive use; their own examples include "HISTORY" for books and "STATISTICAL ANALYSIS" for software ([EUIPO Guidelines, Art. 7(1)(c), 2017 PDF](https://euipo.europa.eu/tunnel-web/secure/webdav/guest/document_library/contentPdfs/trade_marks/Draft_Guidelines_WP_1_2017/08_part_b_examination_section_4_AG_chap_4_article_7-1-c_clean_2017_en.pdf)). The current web edition of the guidelines did not render, so quotations are from the 2017 edition; the principles are unchanged but paragraph numbering may differ.

The Swedish precedents show how an application would actually be handled: as a partial refusal, class by class, not an all-or-nothing outcome. In SOUNDFELT (PMÖD 2017:13) the court refused the mark only for polyester fleece for sound insulation and allowed it for other class 17 goods, asking whether the compound was a "linguistic innovation" whose impression "decisively distances itself from the meaning of its components" ([lagen.nu, PMÖD 2017:13](https://lagen.nu/dom/pmod/2017:13)). In LEGALWORKS (PMÖD 2020:16) the word was refused for legal education but allowed for recruitment and consulting, with the court weighing the need to keep the term free ([lagen.nu, PMÖD 2020:16](https://lagen.nu/dom/pmod/2020:16)). In TRAV OCH GALOPP (PMÄ 4987-25 and 4988-25, 2 October 2025) two ordinary Swedish horse-racing words were descriptive for horse-sport services but not for lotteries or telecom services, and the court required "a clear, direct and concrete link" before refusing ([domstol.se, PMÄ 4988-25](https://www.domstol.se/globalassets/filer/domstol/patentochmarknadsoverdomstolen/avgoranden/2025/pma-4988-25.pdf)). At EU level the closest case is *vita* (T-423/18): EUIPO refused the Swedish word "vita" (white) for kitchen goods on the basis of the Swedish-speaking public alone, and the General Court annulled only because colour is not a characteristic of those goods ([InfoCuria, T-423/18](https://infocuria.curia.europa.eu/tabs/redirect/juris/liste.jsf?num=T-423%2F18&language=EN); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2019/06/vita-descriptive-for-kitchen-goods-although-only-in-sweden)). The full judgment could not be fetched, so paragraph-level reasoning is unverified, but the two lessons stand: EUIPO does refuse on a Swedish meaning alone, and the refusal sticks only where the word names a real characteristic of the goods. "Hardcover" is unmistakably a characteristic of a book, the opposite of the colour situation in *vita*. PRV's own guidance gives "kaffe" for coffee and "tvättmedel" for detergent as unregistrable, states that "alla som säljer kaffe måste dessutom ha rätt att använda ord som 'mörkrostat' och 'brygg'", and cites KEXCHOKLAD as a mark first refused as descriptive and later registered on acquired distinctiveness ([PRV, Särskiljningsförmåga](https://www.prv.se/sv/varumarke/forbered-dig-infor-varumarkesansokan/registreringskrav-for-varumarke/sarskiljningsformaga/)). No PRV, PMÖD or EUIPO decision on INBUNDEN, HARDCOVER, POCKET, HÄFTAD or any other binding word was found; that missing precedent is the single most valuable thing an attorney's database search could add, and the absence of a found decision is not evidence of registrability.

The fallbacks are weaker than they look. A figurative mark in classes 16 and 40 is obtainable, but the EUIPN's Common Practice CP3, which PRV and EUIPO both apply, says a descriptive word in a "basic/standard typeface ... with or without font effects" is not registrable, that the "mere 'addition' of a single colour" adds nothing, that simple frames and word arrangements do not help, and that only a figurative element "distinctive on its own" or stylisation that "depart[s] significantly from the common representation" works; even then "the scope of protection is limited to the overall composition of the mark", so a competitor writing "inbunden fotobok" in plain text is untouchable ([CP3 Practice Paper](https://euipo.europa.eu/tunnel-web/secure/webdav/guest/document_library/EUIPN/Common_practice/CP3/CP3-Practice_Paper_of_Distinctiveness-Figurative_Marks_en.pdf)). Acquired distinctiveness exists on paper (VML 1 kap. 5 § third paragraph; EUTMR Art. 7(3)), but PRV asks for advertisements, first-use dates, sales per category, marketing spend by medium and recognition surveys, and states as its main rule that "ju mer beskrivande ett varumärke är, desto mer krävs"; for an EUTM the proof would be needed in Sweden and Swedish-speaking Finland, which is not a credible plan for a pre-launch app ([PRV, Bevisa förvärvad särskiljningsförmåga](https://www.prv.se/sv/varumarke/forbered-dig-infor-varumarkesansokan/registreringskrav-for-varumarke/sarskiljningsformaga/bevisa-att-varumarket-har-forvarvat-sarskiljningsformaga/)). The company name is caught by the same test: Lag (2018:1653) om företagsnamn 2 kap. 1 § treats a name consisting "enbart av en allmän benämning på verksamhetens art" as non-distinctive, and Bolagsverket refuses names like "Golvexperten AB" and "Webbyrån AB", adding "AB" cures nothing, and suggests "tillägg av ortsnamn, ett fantasiord eller en bokstavs- eller sifferkombination" ([lagen.nu 2018:1653](https://lagen.nu/2018:1653); [Expowera](https://www.expowera.se/starta-eget-foretag/foretagsformer/att-valja-namn-pa-foretaget/om-foretagsnamnet-inte-godkanns); [FAR Online](https://www.faronline.se/dokument/rattserien/ratt-bolagsratt/v/rb_valjaforetagsnamn/)). "Inbunden AB" with a business description covering photo-book production is directly analogous to Golvexperten; with a description limited to software development it is likelier to pass, and "Inbunden Studio AB" or a coined addition is the safe form. Bolagsverket's own guidance page sat behind a CAPTCHA, so its practice is cited through secondary summaries. Filing only in classes 9 and 42 is the cheap defensive step, roughly SEK 3,700 in official fees at PRV or EUR 900 at EUIPO, but it leaves every Swedish printer free to use "inbunden" for its books, and a later physical-book line would be sold under a mark that cannot be registered for those goods.

## Abroad the word is a strong mark, a mild "inbound" collision and a spelling test

Outside the Nordics the legal position flips. In Matratzen Concord (C-421/04) the CJEU held that EU law "does not preclude the registration in a Member State, as a national trade mark, of a term borrowed from the language of another Member State in which it is devoid of distinctive character or descriptive ... unless the relevant parties in the Member State in which registration is sought are capable of identifying the meaning of the term", judged by "average consumers ... reasonably well-informed and reasonably observant and circumspect, in the territory in respect of which registration is applied for"; the German word for mattresses thus stood as a valid Spanish mark for mattresses, and an earlier General Court ruling (T-6/01) even found the German applicant's own mark confusingly similar to it ([ipcuria, C-421/04](https://ipcuria.eu/case?reference=C-421%2F04); [EUR-Lex, T-6/01](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A62001TJ0006_SUM)). Russia's Rospatent applied the same logic to "hygge": because Russian consumers may not understand the Danish word, an earlier HYGGE mark blocked a later one ([Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/trademark-distinctiveness-in-russia-is-hygge-too-good-to-be-a-trademark)). For national filings in Germany, France, Spain or the UK, INBUNDEN is therefore an arbitrary, fully ownable word for photo books; the UK inherits the Matratzen test, though no UKIPO passage on foreign words could be retrieved. The asymmetry is the strategic fact: the mark is weakest where Fredrik launches first and strongest where he expands later.

The United States is a genuine open point. TMEP §1209.03(g) says "foreign words from common, modern languages are translated into English" before descriptiveness is assessed, but the doctrine "is not an absolute rule, but merely a guideline" that applies "only when it is likely that the ordinary American purchaser would stop and translate the foreign wording into its English equivalent"; VEUVE (widow) was held unlikely to be translated in *Palm Bay*, while Japanese, Chinese and Italian terms have been translated and refused ([BitLaw, TMEP 1209.03(g)](https://www.bitlaw.com/source/tmep/1209-03-g.html)). Some circuits go further and treat a term generic in its home country as generic in the US (Otokoyama, "chupa"), while UGG survived despite being generic in Australia ([NYU JIPEL](https://jipel.law.nyu.edu/international-implementation-of-the-doctrine-of-foreign-equivalents-how-to-save-foreign-generic-terms-from-appropriation/)). No USPTO or TTAB decision on a Swedish word was found; whether Swedish counts as a "common, modern language" for this purpose is unverified, and the fallback would be the Supplemental Register or Section 2(f).

The practical international problems are phonetic rather than legal. English speakers will say "in-BUN-den" and, more importantly, hear "inbound"; HubSpot holds US registration 4855780 for INBOUND, ran its INBOUND conference for fifteen years, renamed it UNBOUND for 2026 and still ships the app as `com.hubspot.Inbound2023` ([Justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/866/31/inbound-86631432.html); [Event Marketer](https://www.eventmarketer.com/article/inbound-to-unbound-hubspot-rebrand-conference/); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.hubspot.Inbound2023)). The classes differ (events and marketing software against photo printing), so a legal conflict is unlikely, but "inbound marketing" saturates the SaaS vocabulary a web app lives in, and customers who hear the name will search "inbound photo book". Spelling after hearing is the other cost: "Inbunden", "Inbundin", "Imbunden" and "Inbounden" are all plausible, and none of the misspelling domains was checked. In German the word reads like a past participle beside "verbunden" and "eingebunden" ("bound in", "integrated"), a helpful connotation; Dutch reads it as "ingebonden"-like; French and Spanish speakers will stress the last syllable; no offensive near-word was found in any of these languages, and the only playful English reading is "bun". These perception statements are inference, not survey data; the practitioner standard is three independent native speakers per target language asked about pronunciation, similar words and inappropriate associations ([Operative Words](https://www.operativewords.com/blog/2010/02/red-flags-and-red-herrings-nuances-of.html)). The fluency literature suggests the penalty is small: hard-to-pronounce names are rated riskier and less familiar (Song and Schwarz 2009, with a contested replication) and fluently named IPOs briefly outperform (Alter and Oppenheimer 2006), but a three-syllable word with no diacritics sits far from the Fjällräven end of that scale ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02267.x); [Princeton](https://www.princeton.edu/news/2006/05/29/study-stock-performance-tied-ease-pronouncing-companys-name)). Ironically, the absence of Å, Ä or Ö also removes the Nordic cue: to non-Swedes "Inbunden" reads as vaguely Germanic or invented, so any Scandinavian-design halo, which research on "Nørdic-ness" shows brands actively borrow, would have to come from the design and story rather than the name ([Intervalla, González Rodríguez](https://www.fus.edu/intervalla-files/vol7/7_Gonzalez_Rodriguez_Nordic-ness.pdf); [Wikipedia, Foreign branding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_branding)).

## No Nordic literal-category word has gone global; Klarna dropped one

The precedents Fredrik would be leaning on do not quite fit. Fjällräven ("the arctic fox") has been exported unchanged since the 1960s, reached over 40 countries and derived 71% of revenue from exports as early as 1996, helped by a fox logo that translates the name visually ([Wikipedia, Fjällräven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fj%C3%A4llr%C3%A4ven)). Hövding ("chieftain") kept its Swedish name across Europe and even under its Austrian acquirer iSi Group in 2025 ([Wikipedia, Hövding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B6vding)). IKEA gives every product a real Swedish word of 4 to 12 letters, "preferably containing Å, Ä, or Ö", screened for undesirable foreign meanings, at a rate of 2,000 to 3,000 new names a year ([IKEA](https://www.ikea.com/se/en/customer-service/knowledge/articles/6f564c4d-2ccc-46de-b643-545a3948dc79.html)). In each case the home word is evocative or unrelated to the product: a fox for backpacks, a chieftain for a helmet, a Swedish lake for a bathroom shelf. IKEA's own rule draws the line explicitly: Swedish real words for products, but "for everything else, we use descriptive names in the local language", which means IKEA deliberately does not use Swedish category words as customer-facing labels abroad ([IKEA](https://www.ikea.com/se/en/customer-service/knowledge/articles/6f564c4d-2ccc-46de-b643-545a3948dc79.html)). Klarna is the closest analogue to Fredrik's situation and points the other way: it launched as "Kreditor", a descriptive name, and "in 2009, the company changed the Kreditor name to Klarna" before international scaling, adopting a suggestive Swedish verb form rather than a category noun ([Wikipedia, Klarna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klarna)). Sweden's other global consumer brands (Spotify, whose name came from a misheard shout and a free domain) and Finland's Wolt are coinages ([How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/821019/why-is-it-called-spotify/); [Wikipedia, Wolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolt)). Across the searched material no Nordic brand whose name is the literal category word at home ("hardcover" for a book product) has succeeded internationally; the nearest working model is MATRATZEN in Spain, a word unownable at home and strong abroad, and the nearest publishing-vocabulary brand is Blurb, an adjacent word (cover text) rather than the product noun ([Wikipedia, Blurb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blurb,_Inc.)). The cautionary tales of accidental meanings (Honda Fitta renamed Jazz and Fit for the Nordic launch; Calpis to Calpico; IKEA's Lufsig) show that a small-language collision rarely forces a change unless it is vulgar and in a major market, which is not the case here ([The Local](https://www.thelocal.se/20220210/six-times-swedish-words-unexpectedly-appeared-in-foreign-branding); [Wikipedia, Brand blunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_blunder)). The real risk class for Inbunden is structural weakness at home, not embarrassment abroad.

## Verdict: keep Feedbound as the brand, use Inbunden as the hardcover edition

Inbunden is a workable name and a poor choice as the master brand. Its strengths are real: an empty commercial footprint, four free domains, a warm and slightly witty meaning for Swedes, a helpful "bound in" echo in German and Dutch, and full registrability in every market where Swedish is not understood. Its weaknesses go to the core of the business plan. In Sweden it cannot be a word mark for photo books, printing or retail, it will be outranked in search by its own dictionary meaning, its Instagram handle is held, and the company name needs a distinctive addition; internationally it is heard as "inbound" and has to be spelled out. Feedbound, the leading alternative from the earlier screening, is a coined English compound with a clean register (no company, app or mark in any industry, fuzzy neighbours only FEEDBOND in class 1, SEEDBOUND as a US class 9 application and an ended FEDBOUND), free .app and .io, an apparently free .se, and one material cost: feedbound.com, registered on 30 April 2026 behind Cloudflare with no site ([TMview](https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/#/tmview/results?page=1&pageSize=100&criteria=F&basicSearch=Feedbound); [RDAP](https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/feedbound.com)). Its own costs are that "feed" is Instagram-adjacent vocabulary (compliant with Meta's guidelines, which forbid only "Insta" and "gram", but tied to the platform's meaning) and that it may be heard as "Feedbond" ([Meta Brand Resource Center](https://www.meta.com/brand/resources/instagram/instagram-brand/)).

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. Inbunden is not a clearance problem, it is an ownership problem: the name is free everywhere and protectable in most of the world, but the one jurisdiction where it is unprotectable for books is the one where the company starts, sells, and hopes to add physical hardcovers. The Matratzen logic that makes the word strong in Madrid or Munich is the mirror image of what makes it weak in Stockholm, and no amount of logo design or defensive filing in classes 9 and 42 changes that; only years of documented Swedish use could, and KEXCHOKLAD-scale evidence is not a launch plan.

The productive insight is that a name can be worth using without being worth owning. Every successful Nordic export brand examined here used a word that was already a brand at home, and IKEA reserves its Swedish words for products while describing categories in local language. Applied to Fredrik's case that suggests a two-layer naming system: a coined master brand such as Feedbound that can be registered in every class and every market, and "Inbunden" as the Swedish name of the hardcover edition, where its dictionary meaning does the selling and its unregistrability costs nothing. That split keeps the charm of the Swedish word, avoids the "inbound" collision at the brand level, and leaves the trade-mark budget, about EUR 1,350 for a five-class EU word mark, spent on a name that can actually be defended.

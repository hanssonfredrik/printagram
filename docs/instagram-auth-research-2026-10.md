# Keep the export, pilot Unified Login carefully

Inbunden should keep the Instagram "Download your information" export as the default way in for every user, and treat an API "Connect Instagram" button as an add-on for the minority who already run a Business or Creator account. The reason is structural, not a matter of picking the right login dialog: since Meta shut the Basic Display API on 4 December 2024, no official Instagram API serves personal accounts, and professional accounts cannot be private, so the export is the only route that reaches an ordinary Swedish user with a personal or private profile and still carries photos, captions and dates ([Meta for Developers blog, 4 Sep 2024](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2024/09/04/update-on-instagram-basic-display-api); [Instagram Help: professional accounts](https://help.instagram.com/138925576505882)). The route Inbunden's connect code was built for, Instagram Login on graph.instagram.com, is gone for new apps: Meta's dashboard tells the new Inbunden app that "API setup with Instagram login is not available for newer apps" and offers only Unified Login and Facebook Login (App Dashboard, observed 30 Oct 2026). Of the two that remain, **Unified Login** is the better fit because it needs no Facebook Page, lets the person log in with Instagram credentials, and Venueve AB's already-verified business portfolio satisfies its Business Verification requirement; but it makes every user pick a Meta business portfolio, and Meta's own field reference marks `caption` as "Available for Instagram API with Facebook Login only", which is unconfirmed for Unified Login ([IG Media reference](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/reference/instagram-media.md)). The **Page-linked Facebook Login** route is documented to return captions and like counts, but it asks an Instagram user for a personal Facebook account and a Facebook Page, which no consumer photo-book service was found doing in 2026. Becoming a **Meta data-portability destination** is the one official route that would serve personal accounts without a ZIP, but it is an unpriced, unscheduled vetting programme with no SLA and no confirmation that captions travel with Instagram posts. The recommendation is therefore: ship and polish the export, run two cheap empirical tests on Unified Login (does `caption` come through on a system-user token, and what does the dialog do for a person with no portfolio) before rewriting any connect code, fall back to the Page route only if captions fail, and park the portability route as a watch item.

## No official API has served personal accounts since December 2024

The decisive constraint sits above the choice of login dialog. Meta told developers in September 2024 that "Starting on December 4th, 2024, Instagram Basic Display API will no longer be available" and that the replacements are aimed at "business and creator accounts" ([Meta for Developers blog, 4 Sep 2024](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2024/09/04/update-on-instagram-basic-display-api)). Every surviving setup inherits that rule: "To use the APIs, your app users must have an Instagram professional account", and Unified Login returns "(#10) Access is denied because the instagram account type is not business or creator" for anyone else ([Instagram Platform overview](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/overview/); [Unified Login eligibility and troubleshooting](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/eligibility-and-troubleshooting)). Professional accounts "cannot be private" ([Instagram Help: professional accounts](https://help.instagram.com/138925576505882)), and switching to one "auto-accepts any pending follow requests" ([My Social Book](https://www.mysocialbook.com/blogs/ideas/instagram-books-are-back)). For a private person who keeps a locked account of family photos, every API route therefore starts with "make your account public and professional", which is a real cost even if the switch itself takes about 30 seconds and is reversible.

The industry reacted to this by leaving, not migrating. **Chatbooks** cut Instagram as a source (its access was switched off early on 1 October 2024), called it "impossible for Chatbooks (and all other photo printing companies) to use Instagram as a photo source", and noted that 98 % of its printed photos already came from the camera roll; its help centre still answered "No" to Instagram on 30 September 2026 ([Chatbooks blog, 17 Oct 2024](https://chatbooks.com/blog/changes-to-instagram-new-features); [Chatbooks help, 30 Sep 2026](https://help.chatbooks.com/en/articles/10139802-instagram-as-a-photo-source)). **Day One** considered the business APIs and rejected them because "these solutions don't support personal accounts" ([Day One blog, 6 Dec 2024](https://dayoneapp.com/?p=77355)). Shutterfly's upload guide, updated 16 October 2026, lists Facebook but no longer Instagram, and Mixbook's help titles dropped Instagram while the URL slugs still contain it ([Shutterfly](https://shutterfly.com/ideas/upload-photos); [Mixbook help](https://help.mixbook.com/how-can-i-upload-from-3rd-party-sites-such-as-google-photos-facebook-instagram-and-smugmug-H1hMtMiVB)). The two services that kept a working import took opposite paths: **My Social Book** requires a Professional account, mentions no Facebook Page or Facebook login, and promises captions, dates, locations and likes, which matches the old Instagram Login route on an app created before the change ([My Social Book help, updated 18 Dec 2025](https://www.mysocialbook.com/apps/help/a/how-do-i-create-an-instagram-book); [comparison page](https://www.mysocialbook.com/blogs/ideas/best-photo-book-from-instagram)); **PixxiBook** asks for the Instagram address and requires the profile to be public "at least until the preview has generated", which is public-profile reading rather than any Meta login ([PixxiBook FAQ](https://www.pixxibook.com/doc/faq)). No service in the sample offers an "upload your Instagram export ZIP" route, so Inbunden's existing export importer is an open niche rather than a copy of anyone.

Paid connectors do not change this picture. Ayrshare, Zapier and Make all resell Meta's login and require a Business or Creator account, usually linked to a Facebook Page, and Ayrshare charges $149 to $599 per month for the privilege ([Ayrshare docs](https://www.ayrshare.com/docs/dashboard/connect-social-accounts/instagram.md); [Ayrshare pricing](https://ayrshare.com/pricing); [Zapier help](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/8496101110541)). Scraping a profile, including through a browser extension on the user's own session, is "automated collection without express permission" under Instagram's terms, and the EDPB's 2026 draft guidelines treat scraping of public social media as regulated personal-data processing ([Meta help on scraping](https://help.instagram.com/740480200552298); [EDPB draft guidelines, Jul 2026](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_2020603_webscraping_v1_en_0.pdf)). oEmbed went tokenless on 15 June 2026 but returns only an embed snippet and dimensions after the November 2025 removal of caption, author and thumbnail fields, so it cannot feed a PDF ([Meta for Developers blog, 15 Jun 2026](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2026/06/15/tokenless-access-to-meta-oembed-apis/); [oEmbed Post reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/reference/oembed-post/)). These are excluded, not compared further.

## Instagram Login is closed to new apps, and only the dashboard says so

The route Inbunden's code implements (`https://www.instagram.com/oauth/authorize`, `graph.instagram.com`, scope `instagram_business_basic`, an Instagram app id) was the best consumer fit Meta ever offered after Basic Display: a professional account was enough, no Facebook account, Page or portfolio was needed, and the token model was simple (one-hour short-lived, 60-day long-lived, refreshable after 24 hours) ([Instagram Platform /access_token reference](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/reference/access_token.md); [Business Login for Instagram (Instagram Login)](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-instagram-login/business-login); project source `C:\Projects\Venueve\printagram\api\src\lib\instagramApi.ts`). The new Inbunden app cannot get it. The dashboard banner reads "API setup with Instagram login is not available for newer apps. Your app uses API setup with Unified login instead", and the use case offers only "API setup with Unified login" and "API setup with Facebook login" (App Dashboard, observed 30 Oct 2026). An independent developer hit the same banner on a fresh app on 29 October 2026 and found that new apps have no separate Instagram App ID, that instagram.com authorization returns "Invalid platform app", and that the Facebook dialog rejects `scope` lists as invalid ([Postiz issue #2350](https://github.com/gitroomhq/postiz-app/issues/2350)). Other developers saw the Instagram Login tab vanish on fresh apps as early as 8 October 2026 ([chatmany issue #5](https://github.com/ryanlaiyanip-ctrl/chatmany/issues/5)).

Meta's documentation has not caught up. The platform overview still describes three setups and merely relabels Instagram Login as for "Existing Instagram Login integrations" while recommending Unified Login for "New integrations" ([Instagram Platform overview](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/overview.md)); the migration guide says "This documentation does not announce a retirement date for existing Instagram API setups" ([Unified Login migration](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/migration)); the changelog has no entry about it, and the "Customize the Instagram use case" page still documents only the two legacy tabs ([Instagram Platform changelog](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/changelog); [Customize the Instagram use case](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/development/create-an-app/instagram-use-case)). No support-ticket, transfer or exception path for a new app was found anywhere, and the Create app wizard marks the old "Other" app type as "going away soon" (App Dashboard, observed 30 Oct 2026). The practical conclusion is firm: the existing connect code must be rewritten for a graph.facebook.com setup or parked; it cannot be made to work as written. The only thing that would reopen the old route is an older Meta app that already carries the Instagram Login tab, and Meta documents no way to claim one.

## Unified Login trades the Facebook Page for a business portfolio and an unconfirmed caption

Unified Login is Facebook Login for Business with a Business Integration System User token, and Meta is explicit about who it is for: it "lets your business clients connect their Instagram professional account to your app", the token "belongs to your client's business portfolio, not to the person who logged in", and Facebook Login for Business calls itself the "preferred authentication and authorization solution for tech providers" ([Instagram API with Unified Login](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login); [Facebook Login for Business](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/facebook-login/facebook-login-for-business)). The dashboard's template configuration for Inbunden confirms the shape: login variation General, "System-user access token (SUAT)", expiration "Never (system-user token)", Instagram account as a required asset and ad account as optional, with six permissions preselected (`ads_management`, `ads_read`, `instagram_basic`, `instagram_manage_messages`, `instagram_manage_comments`, `instagram_content_publish`); a custom configuration can be created, and "System-user access token" is described as "Your users will be required to log in using a business portfolio" (App Dashboard, observed 30 Oct 2026).

What the person goes through is documented as **six screens**: log in with Instagram or Facebook, select a business portfolio, select the Instagram professional account (auto-selected if there is only one), select extra assets if requested, review permissions, return to the app ([Unified Login business onboarding](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/business-onboarding)). Two details matter for a consumer audience. First, the Instagram login "opens in the browser, not in the Instagram app, so your client needs their Instagram password", and the Instagram option only appears if the configuration carries the "Instagram Login Compatible" label; `prompt=login` forces both options to show when the browser is already logged in to Facebook ([Unified Login business onboarding](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/business-onboarding); [Unified Login get started](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/get-started)). Second, the portfolio step is a selection, not a creation: the onboarding page contains no occurrence of "create" or "don't have", Meta tells developers to "Tell them before they start that they'll be asked to select a business portfolio", and the only hint of a path for someone without one is the generic Facebook Login for Business line that onboarding businesses "must have, or be willing to create, a business portfolio" ([Unified Login business onboarding](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/business-onboarding); [Facebook Login for Business](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/facebook-login/facebook-login-for-business)). Creating a portfolio outside the dialog is a short form at business.facebook.com (business name, your name, business email, confirmation email) that third-party guides time at "a few minutes" and that needs "access to Meta Business Suite, using either your Facebook profile or a managed Meta account"; verification is not needed to create one ([Leadsie, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.leadsie.com/blog/how-to-create-meta-business-manager-and-facebook-ad-account); [Meta Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1710077379203657)). Adding the Instagram account to the portfolio is another login with username, password and 2FA code inside Business Suite, and an account can sit in only one portfolio ([Meta Help Center via mirror](https://support.chatarchitect.com/books/meta-business-portfolio-setup/page/add-an-instagram-account-to-your-business-portfolio-in-meta-business-suite)). Assembled from those sources, a motivated first-timer who already has a Facebook login spends **roughly 10 to 20 minutes** on the full chain (switch to professional, create portfolio, add the account, redo the dialog) if nothing goes wrong; this is an estimate, since no source times the whole chain. One third-party guide claims Instagram "will automatically set up a new business portfolio for you" when an account is switched to professional, which would make the step invisible for most creators, but a second guide contradicts it and no Meta page says so ([Leadsie on conversion](https://www.leadsie.com/blog/change-instagram-personal-to-professional-account); [Perroly](https://www.perroly.com/tutorials/instagram-facebook-meta-business-suite-prerequisites)). Whether a person with no Facebook profile at all can create a portfolio is unconfirmed; Meta's wording suggests not.

On the data side, the read permission is `instagram_basic` ("Read the Instagram account's profile and media"), the account id comes from `/me/assigned_instagram_accounts`, and media is read from `graph.facebook.com/v26.0/<INSTAGRAM_ACCOUNT_ID>/media` ([Unified Login permissions](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/permissions); [Unified Login authentication](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/authentication)). The IG Media reference lists `like_count`, `media_url`, `media_type`, `timestamp`, `permalink`, `thumbnail_url`, `comments_count` and the `children` edge without any setup restriction, but marks **`caption` as "Available for Instagram API with Facebook Login only"** and adds that "The @ symbol is excluded, unless the app user can perform admin-equivalent tasks on the connected Facebook Page" ([IG Media reference](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/reference/instagram-media.md)). Unified Login uses the same host and permission as Facebook Login, and My Social Book promises captions on what appears to be Instagram Login, so the annotation is probably stale; but Inbunden needs captions, and no Meta sentence confirms they arrive on a system-user token without a Page. This is the single most important thing to test, and Inbunden's import job already logs "how many items had a caption and a like count" per run, so the test costs one tester login (project source `C:\Projects\Venueve\printagram\api\src\functions\instagram.ts`). Expect `media_url` to be missing on some reels with licensed audio and `like_count` to be missing where the owner hides likes; the `/media` edge returns at most 10,000 items ([IG Media reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/reference/instagram-media); [IG User media edge](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/media.md)). Rate limits ("Calls within 24 hours = 4800 * Number of Impressions") are not binding for a one-off import ([Graph API rate limiting](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/overview/rate-limiting)).

The token model is heavier than a one-off photo book needs. The system-user token "Defaults to never expire", can be made to expire in 60 days with `set_token_expires_in_60_days`, is revoked when the client removes the app under Business settings > Integrations > Connected apps or moves the account to another portfolio, and Meta documents no deauthorization webhook for it: "When an access token stops working, stop retrying and ask your client to connect again" ([Facebook Login for Business](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/facebook-login/facebook-login-for-business); [Unified Login authentication](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/authentication)). For a consumer product the right design is the 60-day setting plus app-side deletion once the book is built, which matches the retention promise already drafted for App Review in `docs/meta-app-setup.md`.

Meta review cost is the same shape as for any setup but has two wrinkles. Saving the configuration gives Standard Access, which "lets you test with people who have a role on your app"; serving strangers needs Advanced Access through App Review for "each permission" in the configuration plus Live mode, and Business Verification, which Venueve AB has already completed ([Unified Login permissions](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/permissions); App Dashboard, observed 30 Oct 2026). The first wrinkle is that the get-started page calls both `instagram_basic` and `instagram_manage_messages` "required" in the configuration, while the permissions page lists messaging without that label; if the dashboard forces messaging into a custom configuration, Inbunden would have to request and pass review for a permission it never uses ([Unified Login get started](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login/get-started)). The second is framing: Advanced Access and the system-user token are "Tech Provider" features for serving "business clients", and the dashboard shows a "Become a Tech Provider" card, though Meta retired the separate Access Verification step on 9 October 2026 and says Tech Provider status now "follows from you explicitly upgrading the app, or from the advanced permissions the app itself requests" ([Retiring Access Verification, 9 Oct 2026](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2026/10/09/retiring-access-verification-for-tech-provider-apps/); App Dashboard, observed 30 Oct 2026). The review package itself is a privacy policy, icon, category, per-permission use description and screencast of the full flow with a real Creator account, at least one successful API call per permission within 30 days, and Meta's stated decision time of "within a week" ([App Review submission guide](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/app-review/submission-guide)). Development cost is a rewrite of the OAuth and account-discovery layer in the two Instagram files (new dialog URL with `config_id`, Meta app id and secret instead of Instagram ones, code exchange on graph.facebook.com, `/me/assigned_instagram_accounts`, media on graph.facebook.com, token storage keyed by `client_business_id`), while the import job, blob storage, callbacks and UI stay as they are; the redirect, deauthorize and data-deletion URLs move to Facebook Login for Business > Settings (App Dashboard, observed 30 Oct 2026).

## The Page-linked route returns captions on paper but puts a Facebook Page between a consumer and their photos

The "API setup with Facebook login" tab is still offered to the new app, and the coordinator's dashboard test settles a disputed point: a custom Facebook Login for Business configuration with a **User access token** ("Your users will be required to log in using a personal Facebook account") showed an empty permissions list until `instagram_basic` was added to the use case, after which it could be selected, so the Postiz claim that user-token configurations cannot carry Instagram permissions is wrong (App Dashboard, observed 30 Oct 2026; [Postiz issue #2350](https://github.com/gitroomhq/postiz-app/issues/2350)). A merged PR from 25 October 2026 describes the same thing, a user-token configuration with `pages_show_list`, `pages_read_engagement`, `instagram_basic` and others, invoked with `config_id` ([vision-bright-access PR #506](https://github.com/visionexmanager/vision-bright-access/pull/506)).

What the person must have is heavier than on any other route: a personal Facebook account, a professional Instagram account, a Facebook Page on which they can "perform admin-equivalent tasks", and that Page connected to the Instagram account, because "If your app implements Facebook Login for Business, your app users' Instagram professional accounts must be connected to a Facebook Page" ([Instagram Platform overview](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/overview/)). Meta's "Business Login for Instagram" onboarding dialog (`extras={"setup":{"channel":"IG_API_ONBOARDING"}}`) is advertised as letting users convert, create a Page and connect it "in a single window", but Meta documents it only in outline, its documented URL uses `scope` and `response_type=token` rather than `config_id`, and Facebook Login for Business says "config_id has replaced scope (which should not be used)"; nobody has published a test of the two together ([Business Login for Instagram](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-facebook-login/business-login-for-instagram); [Facebook Login for Business](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/facebook-login/facebook-login-for-business/)). Without that dialog, a user whose Instagram is not linked to a Page completes a Facebook consent screen that offers no Instagram account, and `/me/accounts` comes back without an `instagram_business_account`; integrators describe this as the single most common support case ([Vista Social help](https://support.vistasocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/11327032715291-My-Instagram-profile-is-missing-from-the-connection-screen)). Linking can be done in the Instagram app under Edit profile > "Connect or Create", so the pre-work is a few minutes for someone who already has Facebook; someone who creates a Facebook account for the purpose faces a 5 to 7 day wait before Meta lets a new account connect, per one connector's docs ([GoDaddy help (sv)](https://se.godaddy.com/help/connect-instagram-account-to-facebook-page-32230); [Ayrshare docs](https://www.ayrshare.com/docs/dashboard/connect-social-accounts/instagram.md)).

The payoff is certainty on data. Reading with Facebook Login requires `instagram_basic` plus `pages_read_engagement` (or `pages_show_list` for the `/media` edge) with a Facebook User token, and the reference lists `caption`, `like_count`, `media_url`, `timestamp` and `children` for it without qualification ([IG Media reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-media); [IG User media edge](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/media)). Tokens follow the pattern Inbunden's code already handles: a short-lived user token exchanged server-side for one that "generally lasts about 60 days", plus a 90-day data-access expiry based on when the user was last active ([Long-lived tokens](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/facebook-login/guides/access-tokens/get-long-lived); [Auth vs data access](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/facebook-login/auth-vs-data/)). Review cost is slightly higher than Unified Login because `pages_read_engagement` is a Pages permission reviewers will expect to see justified, and the honest justification ("only to resolve the connected Instagram account") is thin. Development cost is the same size as the Unified rewrite. Strategically, Meta labels this route "Existing Page-linked integrations" and says "New Instagram API features are released only for" Unified Login, so building on it means building on a setup Meta has already marked as legacy ([Instagram Platform overview](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/overview.md); [Instagram API with Unified Login](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-unified-login)).

## The export costs ten taps and a wait, and the portability route is years from consumer-simple

The export is the only route in this report that works for every account type, needs no Meta relationship, and is already built. Instagram's help text says you can request everything you have shared "in a machine readable HTML or JSON format", choosing format, media quality and a date range before tapping Create files, under Accounts Center > Your information and permissions > Download your information ([help.instagram.com](https://help.instagram.com/181231772500920)). Official wording on delivery is "it may take up to 30 days", while third-party observation puts small accounts at 1 to 5 hours and average accounts at 24 to 48 hours, with the download link valid for about four days and a new request allowed once every four days ([help.instagram.com (iPad)](https://help.instagram.com/ipad-app/181231772500920); [MultCloud, 2025](https://www.multcloud.com/tutorials/how-long-does-it-take-to-download-instagram-data-1003-gc.amp.html)). The defaults work against a photo book: format defaults to HTML and media quality to medium, so the guide must tell people to pick JSON and the highest quality ([Uni Bielefeld DONA instructions](https://project05.dona.tf.uni-bielefeld.de/instructions)). The archive carries each post's image, `creation_timestamp` and `title` (the caption, in Latin-1 mojibake that Inbunden's parser already reverses), but **no like counts received**: the export's likes are the ones the user gave, and Inbunden's own UI already says "Likes usually aren't included in the export" (project source `C:\Projects\Venueve\printagram\shared\src\exportSchema.ts` and `C:\Projects\Venueve\printagram\app\src\i18n\en\choose.ts`; [Own Your Memories plugin](https://wordpress.org/plugins/own-your-memories); [MultCloud, Feb 2025](https://www.multcloud.com/explore/what-is-included-in-instagram-data-download-0121-ac.html)). Meta also warns that the archive schema is "not backward compatible" and "regenerated periodically", so the parser needs the kind of fixture tests it already has ([Data Portability changelog](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/data-portability/changelog)). For the user this is roughly ten taps, a wait usually under two days, a ZIP download and an upload; the cost to Inbunden is zero at Meta and ongoing maintenance of the parser.

The same Accounts Center screen now offers a direct transfer instead of a ZIP. Meta's 2026 DMA compliance report describes the consolidated "Export Your Information" tool with "a device export in JSON or HTML, and a direct transfer to external services", fifteen destinations including Google Drive, Dropbox and Google Photos, deep links that let developers "direct users straight into EYI", and "a public portal for developer onboarding" launched in the compliance period ([ppc.land on Meta's DMA report, 6 Mar 2026](https://ppc.land/metas-2026-dma-report-reveals-whatsapp-ads-a-eu200m-fine-and-a-defiant-stance-on-personalized-advertising/)). Becoming a destination is a documented programme: a Level 1 DTI Data Trust Registry entry for photos and posts, a verified Business Manager, an app with the use case "Allow users to transfer their data to other apps", a public privacy policy covering data subject rights, an OAuth 2.0 authorization server, and either a Java adapter contributed to the open-source Data Transfer Project or a "Universal Adapter" HTTP API that Meta calls in a push model; Meta engineers run end-to-end tests, release is requested by emailing dataportability@meta.com, and "there isn't currently a formal SLA" ([Meta data-portability onboarding guide](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/data-portability/onboarding-guide)). The registry's "General Public Use" tier asks for an LEI or DUNS number, a jurisdiction and a security attestation, with no fees or review times published; it left pilot in April 2026 with five small companies named, none of them photo services ([dt-reg.org](https://dt-reg.org/apply-to-registry/); [DTI blog, 28 Apr 2026](https://dtinit.org/blog/2026/04/28/dtr-now-post-pilot)). The data question is open: Meta's "Social Posts" type names "Instagram Posts" and "Photos" names "Instagram Stories and Posts", but no public page lists their fields, Inbunden's own Google Photos route shows that the Photos transfer drops captions and resets dates, and like counts are not a data type at all ([Meta core data types](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/data-portability/core-data-types); project copy in `C:\Projects\Venueve\printagram\app\src\i18n\en\google.ts`). This route could one day give a personal-account user a two-minute flow with no file handling, which is why it deserves a registry application, but it cannot be the 2026 answer.

| EYI destination (data portability) | Programme open, unscheduled | Everyone, via Accounts Center | Deep link into EYI, log in to Inbunden, start transfer, wait for Meta's push | Unconfirmed (Social Posts type) | No | DTI registry Level 1, verified Business Manager, Meta end-to-end test, no SLA or fee published | OAuth 2.0 server plus import API |

## Recommendation: export first for everyone, Unified Login for the connect card only after two tests pass

Keep the export as the default and the first route shown to every visitor, because it is the only one that serves the personal and private accounts most of Inbunden's customers will have, it preserves captions, and it costs nothing at Meta. Tighten the guide with the facts above: pick JSON, pick the highest media quality, expect hours to two days but know Meta's ceiling is 30 days, and likes do not come along. Say the last part plainly so nobody buys a book expecting a "most liked" page from the export.

Do not spend another hour on the Instagram Login code path; it is dead for this app and no one has found a way back. For the "Connect Instagram" card, which is only ever a route for Business and Creator accounts and whose one unique benefit is like counts, choose **Unified Login** over the Page-linked route: it is the setup the dashboard gives the app, it needs no Facebook Page, it offers an Instagram-credentials login, Venueve AB's verified portfolio already covers Business Verification, and Meta ships new features only there. But do it in the order the risks demand. First create a custom configuration with a system-user token, the Instagram account as the only asset, `instagram_basic` as the only permission if the dashboard allows it, and 60-day expiry. Then run the two decisive tests with your own Instagram account as a tester under Standard Access, before rewriting any code: whether `caption` comes back on `/media` (the import log already counts it), and what the dialog shows an Instagram account that belongs to no portfolio. If captions arrive and a portfolio-less user either already has one or can create one inline, rewrite the two Instagram files for Unified Login, submit App Review with the screencast of a real Creator account on inbunden.com, and present connect as the quick path for professional accounts with likes included. If captions are missing, fall back to a user-token configuration on the Page-linked route, where captions are documented, and accept that the card will show a Facebook login and a Page requirement. If the no-portfolio case dead-ends, keep connect as a documented option for professional accounts that already have a portfolio and send everyone else to the export, which the connect flow must do for personal accounts anyway.

Park the data-portability route as a medium-term item with a cheap first step: apply to the DTI registry's General Public tier and ask Meta's portability team whether the Social Posts type carries Instagram caption text. Build nothing until both answers are in. Do not pay for a connector, do not scrape, and do not ship a browser extension.

## Conclusion

The question began as "which login dialog", and the research shows it is really "which users". Meta's post-2024 platform serves businesses and creators through a business-to-business trust model, and every month of 2026 pushed it further that way: Instagram Login vanished for new apps without a documentation update, the dashboard now defaults to a never-expiring portfolio token, and Access Verification was retired only because Business Verification already gates everything. A consumer photo-book service sits outside that model, so the right posture is to treat the API as an optional accelerator for the minority who already live inside Meta's business tooling, and to invest the main effort in the one official door that is open to everyone, the export, where Inbunden is also the only photo-book service with a working importer. The two tests below decide how much the accelerator is worth; neither needs App Review, a code rewrite or more than an afternoon.

What to verify empirically, in this order:

1. **Caption on a system-user token.** Save a Unified Login configuration, add your Instagram account as an Instagram Tester, run one import and read the "instagram import ... with a caption" log line. Zero captions on an account that has them means the reference note is real and the Page route is the fallback.
2. **The no-portfolio dialog.** Open the login URL (with `prompt=login`) from an Instagram professional account that belongs to no business portfolio and record what Meta shows: a create option, a dead end, or an auto-created portfolio as one guide claims.
3. **Minimum permission set.** Check whether a custom configuration can be saved with `instagram_basic` alone or whether the dashboard forces `instagram_manage_messages`, since that decides the App Review scope.
4. **Instagram-only login.** Try the dialog from an account with no Facebook profile and see whether the Instagram option appears ("Instagram Login Compatible") and whether a portfolio can be created from an Instagram-only Business Suite login.
5. **Page route onboarding, only if needed.** Call the Facebook dialog with both `config_id` and `extras={"setup":{"channel":"IG_API_ONBOARDING"}}` and see whether it is accepted, ignored or rejected.
6. **Portability data types.** Email dataportability@meta.com asking whether the Social Posts type includes Instagram caption text and original dates, and note the registry's current fee and review time when applying at dt-reg.org.
